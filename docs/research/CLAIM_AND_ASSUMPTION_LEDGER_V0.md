# World Zero Claim and Assumption Ledger V0

Date: 2026-09-17
Status: seeded research ledger; not exhaustive

## Purpose

A system model can hide assumptions inside equations, lookup tables, initial conditions, aggregation choices and omitted mechanisms. This ledger makes important claims inspectable before they become code.

Each entry separates:

- the proposition;
- its evidence class;
- what evidence currently supports it;
- plausible rival interpretations;
- what World Zero may do with it now;
- what would weaken, reject or supersede it.

`OBSERVED` does not mean causally explained. `INFERRED` does not mean false. `HYPOTHESIS` means the causal proposition has not earned stronger status. `DISPUTED` means credible evidence or interpretation materially diverges.

## Seed ledger

| ID | Proposition | Class | Current basis | Rival / caution | Current model obligation | Kill / downgrade condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C001 | The published Limits to Growth trajectories were conditional scenarios, not precise dated forecasts. | DOCUMENTED-equivalent / OBSERVED_SOURCE | Meadows/Club of Rome descriptions and later retrospectives | Public discussion often treats them as forecasts; individual statements around the work varied | Preserve scenario language; never score the original model as if it predicted exact dates it did not claim | Primary-source evidence showing a specific tested trajectory was presented as an unconditional dated forecast would narrow this claim |
| C002 | Similarity between historical global aggregates and a World3 scenario does not uniquely validate World3 causal structure. | INFERRED / methodological | Multiple structural models can fit low-dimensional aggregate trajectories; identifiability problem | A close fit can still increase plausibility relative to a badly fitting alternative | Historical fit is evidence about trajectory compatibility, not unique causal truth | A discriminating holdout where rival causal structures make distinct predictions and World3 alone survives would strengthen causal credit for that scope |
| C003 | Regional aggregation can hide simultaneous local failure and global adequacy. | HYPOTHESIS strongly motivated by accounting structure | Food, water, income, energy and trade are geographically heterogeneous; global sums do not encode access | Some questions genuinely are global, e.g. atmospheric carbon stock | Regionalize access-constrained systems and aggregate upward | If regionalization adds no holdout or scenario information for a target claim, reduced aggregation may be sufficient |
| C004 | Explicit prices, substitution and investment response can materially alter scarcity dynamics relative to a purely biophysical stock-flow model. | HYPOTHESIS / DISPUTED magnitude | Economic theory and observed substitution/investment responses | Prices cannot create physical supply; low-income demand destruction can look like successful market clearing | Maintain a market-adaptive rival family rather than hard-code strong adaptation into all models | If strong adaptation fails preregistered holdouts or adds no claim-relevant behavior, reduce/remove the mechanism |
| C005 | Technological learning is endogenous enough that fixed technology coefficients can misrepresent long-run transitions. | INFERRED | Historical learning-by-doing and diffusion evidence in multiple technologies | Learning rates are not universal; floors, failure, lock-in and complementary infrastructure matter | Model learning only with saturation/failure/diffusion constraints and rival no-learning cases | If learning-state additions fail holdouts or merely duplicate time trends, treat them as exogenous scenario inputs instead |
| C006 | Gross energy overstates the energy available to the non-energy economy because energy supply consumes energy. | OBSERVED physical accounting; causal macro effect INFERRED | Net-energy/EROI literature; Energy & Environmental Science 2024 review/perspective | EROI definitions depend strongly on system boundaries and energy quality | Track gross/final/useful/net energy with explicit accounting boundaries in the net-energy rival family | If alternative boundary definitions reverse a scenario claim, mark that result structurally/boundary sensitive rather than robust |
| C007 | Economy-wide rebound can materially offset expected energy savings from efficiency improvements. | DISPUTED magnitude / supported mechanism | Reviews and empirical studies find non-zero and sometimes large rebound; estimates vary widely | Estimates range substantially by method, country, horizon and definition; backfire is not universal | Include rebound as uncertain mechanism with low/high/null cases, not a fixed constant | If robust modern estimates for the modeled sectors show negligible rebound, narrow the range; do not preserve a dramatic value for ideological symmetry |
| C008 | Infrastructure replacement and expansion rates impose physical transition constraints separate from technology cost. | OBSERVED / INFERRED generalization | IEA reports multi-year grid, transformer and cable lead times; mines/refineries also have long development cycles | Lead times differ by jurisdiction, technology and period; bottlenecks can ease | Stocks must have age/turnover/build queues or reduced-form delay constraints where material | If a sector can empirically reconfigure on the model timestep with no material queue, do not impose generic slow turnover |
| C009 | Network concentration and topology can make identical aggregate capacity produce different systemic risk. | OBSERVED/INFERRED | Port and supply-chain studies show disruption propagates through network dependencies | Full global network simulation may be unnecessary for many claims | Represent concentration/chokepoint/redundancy at least in the resilient-regional and compound-risk families | If reduced-form concentration metrics reproduce relevant behavior, a full network is not justified |
| C010 | Compound and cascading hazards can produce impacts larger than treating shocks as independent. | OBSERVED concept / INFERRED parameterization | IPCC AR6 and related compound-risk literature | Dependence structure is often data-poor and deeply uncertain | Include independent-shock null plus correlated/cascade rival cases | If dependence cannot be identified, report deep uncertainty rather than fitting a precise joint distribution |
| C011 | Smooth damage functions can miss threshold/regime-shift behavior in ecological and infrastructure systems. | HYPOTHESIS with empirical examples | Tipping/regime-shift literature; observed ecological collapses and infrastructure thresholds | Not every subsystem contains a practically relevant tipping point in the modeled horizon | Threshold mechanisms require system-specific evidence and a smooth null rival | If threshold location/form cannot be bounded and does not alter claims, leave it out of the minimal synthesis |
| C012 | Policy responds to perceived/observed state rather than omniscient true state. | OBSERVED conceptual | Real institutions use delayed/noisy indicators and imperfect models | Some policy rules can be modeled as direct feedback approximations at coarse scale | Distinguish latent system state, measured indicators, beliefs/decision state and policy action where this affects outcomes | If perception dynamics do not change holdouts or target claims, use a simpler lagged policy rule |
| C013 | Income distribution can alter physical consumption patterns even at equal aggregate GDP. | OBSERVED/INFERRED | Consumption baskets and energy/material footprints differ by income and geography | Mapping income to physical demand is dataset- and region-dependent | Distribution module should connect to physical demand/access, not remain a welfare-only diagnostic | If no defensible mapping can be estimated, keep distribution effects bounded and label UNKNOWN rather than invent elasticities |
| C014 | Financial stress can cause contraction before a physical resource becomes absolutely scarce. | OBSERVED historical phenomenon / structural hypothesis for World Zero | Debt crises, credit tightening, inflation and currency stress can suppress investment/consumption | A complete financial system may overtake the project without improving core claims | Start with bounded financing/fiscal constraints and add richer instability only on residual evidence | If finance adds no explanatory/holdout value beyond investment-cost delays, keep it reduced-form |
| C015 | Global population dynamics in the 21st century are dominated by demographic transition and ageing rather than indefinite exponential growth. | OBSERVED projection basis / future uncertain | UN WPP current demographic data and projections | Fertility paths remain uncertain and regional divergence is large | Population cannot be a simple fixed exponential driver; regional age/fertility structure required | Large sustained reversal in fertility/mortality patterns would update the demographic family rather than be treated as model failure alone |
| C016 | Technology and efficiency can reduce material/energy intensity while total throughput still rises if scale grows faster. | OBSERVED identity / empirical question on magnitude | Intensity = throughput/output; arithmetic plus historical evidence of relative decoupling in many cases | Absolute decoupling occurs for some pressures/regions; global and consumption-based boundaries matter | Track intensity and total physical throughput separately | Never infer falling total impact solely from falling intensity |
| C017 | GDP is not a sufficient proxy for either physical throughput or human wellbeing. | OBSERVED definitional / normative boundary | National accounts exclude many non-market outcomes and do not directly measure physical stocks or distribution | GDP remains useful for market-valued production/income | Keep market output, physical throughput and wellbeing diagnostics separate | No single composite should silently become the optimization target |
| C018 | More model detail can reduce scientific quality when parameters become unidentifiable or mechanisms become compensating degrees of freedom. | INFERRED methodological | Equifinality/overparameterization and structural-identifiability literature | Some added mechanisms are necessary even when weakly identified if they enforce conservation or known constraints | Every new mechanism needs admission gates, ablation and identifiability checks | A mechanism with no identifiable effect and no invariant role should remain outside F7 minimal synthesis |

## Source pointers for newly emphasized claims

- Net energy / EROI: Delannoy et al., *Energy & Environmental Science* (2024), DOI 10.1039/D3EE00772C.
- Economy-wide rebound: Brockway et al., *Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews* 141 (2021) 110781; Stern, *Energy Policy* 147 (2020) 111870; Berner et al., *Energy Economics* 110 (2022) 105939.
- Grid/infrastructure lead times: IEA, *Building the Future Transmission Grid* and *World Energy Investment 2025*.
- Critical-mineral constraints: IEA, *Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025*.
- Network disruption: Verschuur, Koks & Hall, *Nature Climate Change* 13 (2023) 804–806.
- Compound/cascading risks: IPCC AR6 WGI Chapter 11 and WGII risk chapters.
- Path dependence/lock-in: Fouquet, *Nature Energy* 1 (2016) 16098 and the wider lock-in literature it reviews.

## Ledger governance

New claims should be appended or explicitly supersede prior entries. Do not rewrite an old proposition merely because later evidence changes its status. A revision should identify the prior claim ID, the new evidence, the new class/status and the reason for the change.

A model equation is not allowed to become the first place an important assumption appears.
