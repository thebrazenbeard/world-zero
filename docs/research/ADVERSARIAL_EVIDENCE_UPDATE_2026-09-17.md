# World Zero Adversarial Evidence Update — 2026-09-17

Status: research input; does not automatically admit mechanisms into F7

## Purpose

This update records sources consulted after the first World Zero extension matrix was challenged for adding modern sectors without sufficiently challenging inherited causal assumptions.

The sources below justify **testing** mechanisms. They do not by themselves establish exact World Zero equations or parameter values.

## Net energy / EROI

Source:

- Delannoy et al. (2024), “Emerging consensus on net energy paves the way for improved integrated assessment modeling,” *Energy & Environmental Science* 17, 11–26. DOI: 10.1039/D3EE00772C. https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2024/ee/d3ee00772c

Source-supported points:

- extracting, processing and delivering energy consumes energy;
- net energy differs from gross energy supply;
- the authors argue that many mitigation/transition models overlook net-energy feedbacks;
- EROI results depend on system boundaries and energy stage;
- transition investment itself can temporarily consume substantial energy.

World Zero implication:

- create F3 net-energy/material rival structure;
- preserve gross/final/useful/net energy distinctions;
- treat EROI boundary definition as structural uncertainty rather than one universal scalar.

Not established by this source alone:

- a unique global EROI trajectory;
- a deterministic economic-growth response to EROI;
- that net-energy constraints dominate all other transition constraints.

## Economy-wide rebound

Sources:

- Brockway et al. (2021), “Energy efficiency and economy-wide rebound effects: A review of the evidence and its implications,” *Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews* 141, 110781. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1364032121000769
- Stern (2020), “How large is the economy-wide rebound effect?”, *Energy Policy* 147, 111870. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0301421520305863
- Berner et al. (2022), “Do energy efficiency improvements reduce energy use? Empirical evidence on the economy-wide rebound effect in Europe and the United States,” *Energy Economics* 110, 105939. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S014098832200113X

Source-supported points:

- direct, indirect and economy-wide rebound mechanisms are distinct;
- published estimates vary substantially by method and scope;
- several reviews/studies find potentially large economy-wide rebound;
- there is no defensible basis for assuming rebound is universally zero or universally 100%.

World Zero implication:

- represent rebound as a rival/null/low/high structural hypothesis;
- bind parameter ranges to sector, region, horizon and evidence cut;
- do not use “Jevons paradox” as a universal law.

## Infrastructure turnover and deployment bottlenecks

Sources:

- IEA, *Building the Future Transmission Grid* executive summary. https://www.iea.org/reports/building-the-future-transmission-grid/executive-summary
- IEA, *World Energy Investment 2025* executive summary. https://www.iea.org/reports/world-energy-investment-2025/executive-summary

Source-supported points:

- transmission expansion faces permitting and supply-chain delays;
- IEA reported cable procurement on the order of years and large-transformer procurement up to several years in its survey;
- grid investment can lag generation investment;
- physical component supply and utility finances can constrain deployment independently of generation-technology cost.

World Zero implication:

- desired investment must not instantaneously become installed capacity;
- model slow capital using construction/retirement queues or validated reduced-form delays;
- keep jurisdiction/technology-specific lead times data-bound rather than universal.

## Critical-mineral bottlenecks

Source:

- IEA, *Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025* executive summary. https://www.iea.org/reports/global-critical-minerals-outlook-2025/executive-summary

Source-supported points:

- mineral constraints are material-specific;
- copper and lithium outlooks differ from nickel/cobalt/graphite/rare earths;
- project pipelines, ore grades, capital costs, discoveries, lead times and geographic concentration matter;
- present apparent market balance does not remove future concentration/export-control risk.

World Zero implication:

- do not use one generic “critical mineral” depletion curve for all claims;
- V0 may use a compact basket, but the basket must retain at least distinct supply/processing/recycling behavior for minerals that drive different constraints;
- concentration and lead-time sensitivity belong in hostile cases.

## Network topology and chokepoints

Source:

- Verschuur, Koks & Hall (2023), “Systemic risks from climate-related disruptions at ports,” *Nature Climate Change* 13, 804–806. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41558-023-01754-w

Source-supported points:

- climate-related port downtime can propagate through shipping, trade and supply-chain networks;
- systemic exposure depends on network relationships, not only local asset loss;
- identical aggregate transport capacity can therefore imply different systemic vulnerability depending on concentration and routing.

World Zero implication:

- maintain an aggregate-capacity null model;
- test concentration-aware reduced-form topology before building a full global graph;
- only promote detailed network structure when it improves a target holdout/claim.

## Path dependence and lock-in

Source:

- Fouquet (2016), “Path dependence in energy systems and economic development,” *Nature Energy* 1, 16098. https://www.nature.com/articles/nenergy201698

Source-supported points:

- energy systems can exhibit long-lived technological, infrastructural, institutional and behavioral lock-in;
- early infrastructure/policy choices can shape later transition possibilities.

World Zero implication:

- endogenous innovation cannot be only a downward cost curve;
- competing technologies require installed-base inertia, complementary infrastructure and adoption/diffusion delays;
- “best technology wins immediately” is a hostile-test failure mode.

## Compound and cascading risk

Sources:

- IPCC AR6 WGI Chapter 11, compound events. https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-11/
- IPCC AR6 WGII Chapter 1, complex risk. https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/chapter/chapter-1/
- IPCC AR6 WGII Chapter 6, compound/cascading risks in settlements and infrastructure. https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/chapter/chapter-6/

Source-supported points:

- compound events can involve simultaneous, successive, spatially connected or preconditioned hazards;
- impacts can exceed the sum of isolated impacts because vulnerabilities and coping limits interact;
- dependence structures can be deeply uncertain and data-poor.

World Zero implication:

- use independent-shock behavior as a null;
- support correlated/conditional cascade structures in F6;
- deep uncertainty should widen/branch scenarios rather than be disguised as a precise joint probability.

## Research posture after this update

The evidence supports adding **tests and rival structures** for these mechanisms.

It does not support skipping the mechanism-admission gates.

For each mechanism World Zero still needs:

- exact observation mapping;
- identifiable or explicitly non-identifiable parameterization;
- historical/holdout target;
- rival/null structure;
- ablation;
- numerical stress test;
- claim-specific structural comparison.

The correct response to plausible but underidentified mechanisms is `FAMILY_SENSITIVE`, `IDENTIFICATION_LIMITED` or `UNKNOWN`, not arbitrary certainty.
