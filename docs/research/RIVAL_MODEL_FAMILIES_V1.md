# World Zero Rival Model Families V1

Date: 2026-09-17
Status: research/design contract

## Purpose

World Zero must not become one causal worldview with many parameters. It must support competing frozen model families that share an observation layer, dataset manifests, run receipts, validation protocol and comparable outputs while differing in causal structure.

The purpose is not to crown a philosophical winner. It is to identify which mechanisms are necessary, which are observationally redundant, which improve holdout behavior, and which materially change conditional scenario conclusions.

## Common invariants

Every rival family must:

- bind an exact source commit and topology digest;
- use the same admitted historical data cut for a declared comparison;
- expose the same comparison observables where the family can meaningfully produce them;
- declare every mechanism as endogenous, exogenous, intervention-controlled or absent;
- preserve units, conservation constraints and regional accounting;
- report parameter uncertainty separately from structural uncertainty;
- run the same temporal, regional and variable holdouts unless a declared incompatibility makes a holdout impossible;
- run the same numerical-convergence and solver-sensitivity suite;
- preserve failed and superseded subjects rather than rewriting them;
- receive no credit for fitting a variable it was calibrated directly against unless the qualification claim says so explicitly.

A family may be simpler than another. Complexity is not a virtue. A simpler family that survives the same hostile tests should be preferred for the claim in question until additional structure earns its keep.

## Family F0 — World3-compatible biophysical constraint

Purpose: frozen control subject preserving the classical stock-flow logic closely enough to ask whether newer structures actually improve explanatory or scenario performance.

Emphasis:

- population;
- industrial/service capital;
- agriculture;
- nonrenewable resources;
- persistent pollution;
- delays and reinforcing/balancing feedbacks.

Deliberately weak or absent:

- explicit market prices;
- endogenous innovation competition;
- detailed regional trade;
- network cascades;
- detailed net-energy accounting.

Kill question: if modern families cannot outperform this control on preregistered holdouts or materially improve causal discrimination, the extra complexity has not earned inclusion.

## Family F1 — Market-adaptive

Purpose: test the criticism that scarcity responses, substitution, trade and investment adaptation make World3-style overshoot materially too pessimistic.

Required mechanisms:

- scarcity-sensitive prices;
- demand response;
- substitution elasticities;
- exploration/resource-development response;
- trade reallocation;
- investment redirection;
- affordability/distribution constraints;
- rationing or shortage behavior where prices cannot clear physical scarcity.

Required hostile controls:

- near-zero substitution;
- high substitution;
- rapid versus delayed investment;
- concentrated versus diversified supply;
- price spikes with low-income demand destruction.

Failure condition: the family may not treat prices as creating physical supply. Market clearing is not a conservation-law exemption.

## Family F2 — Endogenous innovation

Purpose: test whether induced innovation, learning, diffusion and technological substitution materially change long-run constraints.

Required mechanisms:

- R&D allocation or innovation effort;
- competing technology families;
- cumulative-learning effects;
- diffusion/adoption delay;
- infrastructure complementarity;
- failure/attrition probability;
- saturation or floor costs;
- skills/manufacturing constraints;
- path dependence and lock-in.

Required hostile controls:

- stalled learning;
- failed candidate technologies;
- infrastructure bottlenecks;
- rebound/induced demand;
- incumbent lock-in;
- accelerated breakthrough scenario explicitly labeled as intervention/scenario, not historical fact.

Failure condition: a monotonic cost curve with no failure, diffusion or infrastructure constraint does not qualify as endogenous innovation.

## Family F3 — Net-energy and material bottleneck

Purpose: test whether gross energy/resource accounting hides transition burdens and declining useful surplus.

Required mechanisms:

- gross primary/final/useful energy distinction;
- energy consumed by energy extraction/conversion/delivery;
- declared EROI or equivalent net-energy accounting boundaries;
- energy required to build replacement/transition infrastructure;
- mineral extraction/refining/recycling constraints;
- ore-grade or quality effects where supported;
- construction lead times.

Required hostile controls:

- alternative EROI boundary definitions;
- high/low transition-energy burden;
- rapid/slow recycling maturation;
- copper/lithium bottleneck variations;
- demand-side reduction versus constant-service-demand cases.

Failure condition: EROI may not be represented as one timeless global scalar. Boundary definition and energy carrier quality must remain explicit.

## Family F4 — Institutional-fragility

Purpose: test whether state capacity, information quality, inequality, legitimacy and coordination failure dominate outcomes that physical-capital models attribute to resource scarcity alone.

Required mechanisms:

- observation/perception lag;
- policy-response delay;
- implementation capacity;
- fiscal capacity;
- inequality/distribution feedback;
- trust/legitimacy or an explicitly scoped proxy;
- coordination capacity;
- policy reversal/error.

Required hostile controls:

- competent fast response;
- delayed response;
- wrong-model policy response;
- fiscal stress;
- high versus low inequality;
- cross-border coordination failure.

Failure condition: institutions cannot be one latent 'good governance' knob whose direction is chosen to make scenarios behave.

## Family F5 — Resilient regional network

Purpose: test whether global aggregation creates artificial collapse by hiding substitution, migration, trade, redundancy and asynchronous regional dynamics.

Required mechanisms:

- macroregional stocks/flows;
- bilateral or reduced-form networked trade;
- import dependence and supplier concentration;
- migration with global population conservation;
- regional adaptation capacity;
- regional failure without automatic global failure;
- spare capacity/redundancy where evidence supports it.

Required hostile controls:

- hub failure;
- trade fragmentation;
- rerouting capacity;
- regional crop shock;
- migration constraint;
- highly concentrated versus diversified network topology.

Failure condition: the model may not average away regional starvation, power shortage or institutional failure merely because the global sum remains adequate.

## Family F6 — Compound-risk cascade

Purpose: test whether correlated shocks and nonlinear cascades make smooth deterministic scenarios structurally too optimistic.

Required mechanisms:

- conditional/correlated shocks;
- cross-sector propagation;
- recovery time and repair capacity;
- threshold-triggered regime changes where justified;
- infrastructure dependencies;
- tail-event ensembles.

Required hostile controls:

- independent-shock null model;
- correlated drought/heat/crop cases;
- energy-trade-finance cascade;
- port/logistics disruption;
- repeated shocks before full recovery;
- alternative dependency graphs.

Failure condition: this family cannot simply add larger random noise. Cascade structure must be explicit enough to identify the propagation path.

## Family F7 — Minimal adaptive synthesis

Purpose: provide a deliberately conservative candidate that includes only mechanisms that survive mechanism-admission gates across the previous families.

This is not the presumptive final model. It is assembled only after component evidence exists.

A mechanism enters F7 only if it has:

1. a declared empirical or structural motivation;
2. an identifiable or explicitly uncertain parameterization;
3. a preregistered kill test;
4. evidence that it changes an important residual, holdout result or scenario claim;
5. no simpler substitute with equivalent performance.

## Comparison protocol

For each exact data cut, compare families across multiple dimensions rather than one composite score:

- temporal holdout error;
- regional holdout error;
- variable holdout error;
- turning-point timing;
- direction-of-change accuracy;
- conservation/invariant violations;
- residual autocorrelation and systematic bias;
- uncertainty coverage;
- shock/recovery behavior;
- sensitivity concentration;
- identifiability diagnostics;
- claim stability under plausible structural alternatives.

No family receives a universal ranking. Results are claim-specific. A family may be better for energy-system transition behavior and worse for demographic or regional outcomes.

## Structural uncertainty output

A World Zero result must be able to say:

- `ROBUST_ACROSS_FAMILIES` — the stated directional/qualitative result survives the declared rival set;
- `FAMILY_SENSITIVE` — materially different results arise under plausible rival structures;
- `IDENTIFICATION_LIMITED` — available evidence cannot distinguish rival structures sufficiently;
- `CONTROL_ONLY` — result is currently established only in the World3-compatible control or another single family;
- `UNKNOWN` — evidence is inadequate for a stronger statement.

These labels describe the tested scope only. They are not claims of universal predictive truth.
